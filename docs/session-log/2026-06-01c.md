## 3  Technical Decisions

**Y-Z view for panel (b), not X-Y.**  The periapsis is at (dx≈−15, y≈0, z≈+283 km)
— nearly directly above Enceladus's north pole along the X-axis.  In the X-Y
top-down projection the spacecraft appears to pass through the body (projected
distance from origin is only 14.9 km vs R_enc=252.1 km).  The Y-Z view (looking
along −X) correctly shows the 31-km clearance above the limb.

**All 3 loops geometrically superimposed.**  Our CR3BP period-3 IC is at the
period-tripling bifurcation point, so all 3 loops trace the exact same 3D path
(confirmed by numerical comparison: Δ < 1 km at every equivalent phase).  They
differ only in time (12 hr apart).  The MacKenzie figure shows 3 distinct periapsis
longitudes because their higher-fidelity orbit (JPL ephemeris + full gravity) breaks
this degeneracy.  Our figure honestly labels this in the subplot titles.

**Altitude colourmap (plasma_r) for both trajectory panels.**  Dark purple = apoapsis
(~1065 km), bright yellow = periapsis (31 km).  This lets the viewer immediately see
where the spacecraft is low-altitude without separate scatter plots.

**J2 escape is expected physics, not a bug.**  The uncontrolled CR3BP+J2 orbit escapes
within 2 revolutions because the period-3 orbit sits near the period-tripling
bifurcation and is highly unstable; J2 breaks the CR3BP symmetry that sustains it.
Panel (c) deliberately shows this — it motivates the POMDP stationkeeping controller.

## 5  Known Issues / Blockers

- **3 periapsis passes co-located — body-fixed frame does NOT help.**
  Initially suspected a frame-rotation fix (Enceladus spin vs CR3BP frame). Investigated
  and confirmed: Enceladus is tidally locked, so its body-fixed IAU frame IS the CR3BP
  rotating frame. Rotating coordinates does not spread the 3 passes.

  Numerically verified: all 3 passes land at lat≈87°N, lon≈{-164°, -173°, -156°} —
  only ~16° spread, essentially the same polar point. MacKenzie's figure shows passes
  ~120° apart in longitude, which requires the orbit itself to have 3 geometrically
  distinct 3D loops — a **non-symmetric period-3 orbit** found by multiple shooting in
  a full JPL ephemeris model (no y=0 symmetry constraint). Our symmetric bifurcation IC
  forces all 3 loops to be identical copies (y=0 at start/end, same x-z path).

  To reproduce MacKenzie panel (b) exactly: need a 6-variable multiple-shooting corrector
  with no symmetry, seeded from the JPL ephemeris catalog. Significant work — at least
  one dedicated session. Logged in todo.md as a future item.
- **J2 rapid escape**: orbit escapes in ~2 revolutions without stationkeeping. This is
  correct physics but means panel (c) only shows 4 J2 data points. A stationkeeping
  controller (Phase 4+) will produce the full 36-day panel.
