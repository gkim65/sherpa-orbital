## 3  Technical Decisions

**Updated constants to JPL DE440.** The JPL Three-Body Periodic Orbits Catalog
(the source of the MacKenzie orbit) uses LU=238529 km and TU=18913.28 s.  Our
old values (Jacobson 2006: LU=238020 km) caused a 0.2% unit mismatch that prevented
the catalog IC from closing in our integrator.  After the update the JPL IC closes
to 2.9 km over 36 hr (before correction) and 0.0017 km after one Newton step.

**Period-3 orbit physics.** The science orbit is not a simple L1 halo — it is a
period-3 resonant orbit.  From MacKenzie §B.2.3: "12-hour period between subsequent
passages of periapsis."  The full orbit repeats after 3 loops = 36 hr.  Key implication
for the corrector: `n_crossings=2` propagates to the 2nd descending y=0 crossing
(t ≈ 18 hr = half of the 36-hr period), which is the correct symmetry condition.

**JPL catalog IC.** The period-3 IC was taken directly from the JPL Three-Body Periodic
Orbits Catalog (DE440, southern L1 halo family, periapsis ~31 km amplitude):
  x0 = 9.974083488926582e-01 nondim
  z0 = -4.874948479304304e-03 nondim  (south polar, z < 0)
  vy0 = 5.466912598254453e-03 nondim

This is already a period-3 fixed point: in the JPL system, the monodromy matrix
eigenvalues are exp(±i·120.6°) — essentially at the period-tripling bifurcation.
In our system (after constant update) the IC converges in 1 Newton iteration.

**Richardson approximation limitations.** For the Enceladus CR3BP, the Richardson
(1980) Lindstedt-Poincaré expansion is invalid for the mission orbit because
Az/gamma1 ≈ 0.42 >> 0.1 (the valid range). We retain the implementation for
reference and for small-amplitude orbits, but it cannot seed the mission orbit.

**MacKenzie methodology.** MacKenzie et al. did not derive the CR3BP IC from scratch —
they had it from prior work (likely the JPL catalog or Hernandez et al., cited B-25)
and used multiple-shooting in a higher-fidelity model to refine it.  Our single-shooting
corrector is appropriate for the CR3BP-only case.

## 4  Physics / Math Notes

**Period-3 orbit structure:**
- Total period: 36 hr = 3 × 12 hr between periapses
- Each loop: periapsis at ~31 km altitude, apoapsis at ~1065 km altitude
- 3 periapsis passes distributed over Enceladus south polar terrain (Tiger Stripes)
- Southern halo: z < 0 at IC (spacecraft starts north of equatorial plane, periapsis south)
- Monodromy eigenvalues at IC: exp(±i·120.6°) — very near period-3 bifurcation point
